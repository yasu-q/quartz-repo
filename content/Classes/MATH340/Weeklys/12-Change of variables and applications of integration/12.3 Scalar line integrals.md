Suppose some curve is given by the parametrization ${\bf x} : [a, b] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2$. Let $\delta(x,y)$ be density function of this curve. If this curve is partitioned into pieces of length $\Delta s_i$, then the total mass of this curve can be approximated via
$$
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n} \delta({\bf x}(t_i)) \Delta s_i
$$
By the way, $\Delta s_i$ is just the arc length. As $\Delta s_i$ approaches zero i.e. as the length that we are adding up becomes increasingly smaller, we obtain an integral
$$
\int\limits_{C} \delta(x,y) \ ds
$$
Where $C$ is the curve, and arc-length ($ds$) is given by (see  [[7.4 Curves in Rn (derivatives)]])
$$
\int || {\bf x}'(t) || \ dt
$$
## 12.7 Scalar line integrals
Consider some curve $C$ given by a parametrization ${\bf x} : [a,b] \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^n$. The scalar line integral of a real valued function $f$ over this curve is given by
$$
\int\limits_C f \ ds = \int\limits_{a}^{b} f\circ{\bf x}(t) \ || {\bf x}'(t)|| \ dt
$$

## Example 12.9
Find the total mass of a wire located at the unit circle $x^2+y^2 = 1$ whose density function is $\delta(x,y) = x^2$

First we parametrize this curve with 
$$
x(t) = \cos(t) 
\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,
y(t) = \sin(t)
$$
With $t \in [0, 2\pi]$. Find the arc-length ($ds$) of this parametrization. 
$$
(x'(t),y'(t)) = (-\sin t, \cos t)
$$
$ds$ is then
$$
|| (-\sin t, \cos t) || = \sqrt{(-\sin t)^2 + (\cos t)^2} = \sqrt{\sin^2 t + \cos^2 t} = 1
$$
Yielding a final integral of 
$$
\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi} \delta(\cos t, \sin t) \ dt = \int\limits_{0}^{2\pi} \cos^2t \ dt
$$
Utilizing the power reduction formula for cosine
$$
\Rightarrow \frac{1}{2} \int\limits_{0}^{2\pi} 1 + \cos 2t \ dt
$$
Which evaluates to 
$$
\frac{1}{2} \Big[t + \frac{1}{2} \sin(t) \Big|_{t=0}^{t=2\pi} = \frac{1}{2}(2\pi) = \pi
$$
So the mass is $\pi$

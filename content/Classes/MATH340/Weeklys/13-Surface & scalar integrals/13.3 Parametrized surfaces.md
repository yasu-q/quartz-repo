## 13.7 Parametrized surfaces
Let $D$ be a subset of $\mathbb{R}^2$ that consists of an open connected set along with some or all of its boundary. A parametrized surface in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is a continuous function ${\bf X} : D → \mathbb{R}^3$ that is one-to-one on $D$ except possibly along $∂D$. We say ${\bf X}(D)$ is a surface parametrized by $X$.

## Example 13.6
Find the parametrization for the unit sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 1$ and the cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 1$

For the sphere, we can simply use the spherical coordinate system (see [[12.1 Change of variables]]) which are
$$
x = \rho \sin(\theta) \cos(\varphi) \,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,
y = \rho \sin(\theta) \sin(\varphi) \,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,
z = \rho \cos(\varphi) 
$$
Since the radius is $\rho = 1$, we can parametrize the sphere using
$$
{\bf X}(\theta, \varphi) = (\sin\theta \cos\varphi, \sin\theta\sin\varphi, \cos\varphi)
$$
For $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$ and $\varphi \in [0, \pi]$

Similarly, we can just use cylindrical coordinates (see [[12.1 Change of variables]]) to parametrize the cylinder. In this case
$$
x = r\cos(\theta) \,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,
y = r\sin(\theta) \,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,
z = z
$$
Since $r=1$, we can use
$$
{\bf X}(\theta, z) = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta, z)
$$
For $\theta \in [0, 2\pi]$

## 13.9 Smooth surfaces
The parametrized surface $S = {\bf X}(D)$ is said to be smooth at ${\bf X}(s_0, t_0)$ if ${\bf X}$ is continuously differentiable on an open ball around $(s_0, t_0)$ and the vector
$$
{\bf N}(s_0, t_0) = X_s(s_0, y_0) \times X_t(s_0, t_0)
$$
is nonzero. If $S$ is smooth at every point, then we say ${\bf X}$ is a smooth parametrization of $S$. The vector $N(s_0, t_0)$ is called the standard normal vector arising from ${\bf X}$.
## 13.10 Piecewise smooth surfaces
A piecewise smooth parametrized surface is the union of images of finitely many parametrized surface $X_i : D_i → \mathbb{R}^3$, where

1. Each $D_i$ is a region in $\mathbb{R}^2$ consisting of a connected open set, possibly together with some or all of its boundary points

2. Each $X_i$ is continuously differentiable

3. Each $X_i$ is one-to-one (doesn't overlap itself) except possibly on the boundary of $D_i$.

4. Each $X_i(D_i)$ is smooth except possibly at finitely many points or points of its boundary

## Theorem 13.7
Suppose $X(s, t)$ with $X : D → \mathbb{R}^3$ is a piecewise smooth parametrization of a surface $S$. Then the area of $S$ is evaluate by
$$
\iint_D || X_s \times X_t || \ dA
$$

## Example 13.9
Find the surface area of a sphere of radius $a$

Lets first parametrize the sphere. We know a way to do this is
$$
X(s,t) = (a \sin s \cos t, a \sin s \sin t, a \cos t)
$$
For $s \in [0, 2\pi]$ and $t \in [0, \pi]$. Then, we will utilize
$$
\iint_D || X_s \times X_t || \ dA
$$
I won't compute the cross product, but just know the general formula for this spherical cross product is
$$
\rho^2 \sin \varphi (\rho \sin\theta \cos \varphi, \rho \sin \theta \sin \varphi, \rho \cos \varphi)
$$
Which is just the parametrization times the Jacobian spherical constant (see [[12.1 Change of variables]]) for some reason. When you compute the magnitude of this vector, you get
$$
a^2 \sin t
$$
Which is the integrand. Then, set up the integral
$$
\int\limits_{0}^{\pi} \int\limits_{0}^{2\pi} a^2 \sin t \ ds \ dt
$$
Which computes to $4\pi a^2$

### Another parametrization
The surface given by $z = f(x,y)$ (i.e. the graph of a function $f(x,y)$) can be parametrized with
$$
X(x,y) = (x,y, f(x,y))
$$
At which point
$$
X_x \times X_y = (-f_x, -f_y, 1)
$$

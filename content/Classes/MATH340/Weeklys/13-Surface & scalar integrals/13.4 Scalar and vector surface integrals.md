## 14.11 Scalar surface integrals
 Let $D ⊆ \mathbb{R}^2$ be a bounded region. Let $X : D → \mathbb{R}^3$ be a piecewise smooth parametrized surface. Let $f$ be a continuous real-valued function whose domain contains $S = X(D)$. Then the scalar surface integral of $f$ along $X$ is given by
$$
\iint_X f \ dS = \iint_D f \circ X(s,t) \, || {\bf N}(s,t) || \ dA
=
\iint_D f \circ X(s,t) \, || X_s \times X_t || \ dA
$$
See [[13.3 Parametrized surfaces]]

## Example 13.10
Let $S$ be the paraboloid $z = x^2 + y^2 - 4$ below the $xy$ plane. Evaluate $z + 4$ over this surface.

This thing. Since this is the graph of a function, we can parametrize this using
$$
X(x,y) = (x,y, x^2 + y^2 - 4)
$$
(see [[13.3 Parametrized surfaces]]). We know 
$$
X_x \times X_y = (-f_x, -f_y, 1) = (-2x, -2y, 1)
$$
The magnitude of this is then
$$
|| (-2x, -2y, 1) || = \sqrt{(-2x)^2 + (-2y)^2 + 1^1} = \sqrt{4x^2 + 4y^2 + 1}
$$
Since $f(x,y,z) = z+4$, we have that
$$
f \circ X(x,y) = x^2 + y^2 - 4 + 4 = x^2 + y^2
$$
Putting everything together in the double integral yields
$$
\iint_D x^2 + y^2 \sqrt{4x^2 + 4y^2 + 1} \ dA
$$
Note that the projection of $D$ onto the $xy$ plane is a circle of radius $2$, thus we can use polar coordinates to evaluate this integral. 
$$
r^2 = x^2 + y^2
$$
And the Jacobian factor is $r$ (see [[12.1 Change of variables]]). This gives the integral
$$
\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi} \int\limits_{0}^{2} r^2 \sqrt{4r^2 + 1} r \ dr \ d\theta
$$
For the internal integral, we use $u = 4r^2 + 1$ where $du = 8r \ dr$
$$
\int r^2 \sqrt{u} r \ \frac{du}{8r}
$$
We can solve for $r^2$ in terms of $u$ which is $r^2 = \frac{u-1}{4}$ 
$$
\frac{1}{8} \int \frac{u-1}{4} \sqrt{u}  \ du
\,\,\, \Rightarrow \,\,\,
\frac{1}{32} \int u^{\frac{3}{2}} - u^{\frac{1}{2}} \ du
\,\,\, \Rightarrow \,\,\, \frac{1}{32} \left[ \frac{2}{5}u^{\frac{5}{2}} - \frac{2}{3}u^{\frac{3}{2}} \right]_{u=??}^{u=??}
$$
Sub everything back or just change the bounds and you get some number then just integrate that from $0$ to $2\pi$ and ur done $\square$.

## 13.12 Vector surface integrals
 Let $D ⊆ \mathbb{R}^2$ be a bounded region. Let $X : D → \mathbb{R}^3$ be a piecewise smooth parametrized surface. Let ${\bf F}(x,y,z)$ be a continuous real-valued function whose domain contains $S = X(D)$. Then the vector surface integral of ${\bf F}$ along $X$ is given by
$$
\iint_X {\bf F} \ d{\bf S} = \iint_D {\bf F} \circ X(s,t) \, \cdot {\bf N}(s,t) \ dA
=
\iint_D {\bf F} \circ X(s,t) \, \cdot (X_s \times X_t)  \ dA
$$
Yes these surface integral definitions are like basically the same thing as the line integral ones except you have these cross products and parametrized surfaces.

Because this is the case, a lot of the theorems and properties of line integrals apply to these as well.

## 13.13 Smooth reparameterizations
Let $D_1$, $D_2$ be two regions in $\mathbb{R}^2$; $X_1 : D_1 → \mathbb{R}^3$, and $X_2 : D_2 → \mathbb{R}^3$ be two parametrized surfaces. We say $X_2$ is a reparameterization of $X_1$ if there is a bijection $H : D_2 → D_1$ such that $X_2 = X_1 \circ H$. if $X_1$ and $X_2$ are piecewise smooth and $H$ and $H^{−1}$ are continuously differentiable we say $X_2$ is a smooth reparameterization of $X$

## 13.14 Orientable surfaces
A smooth, connected surface $S$ is called orientable if it is possible to define a unit normal vector at each point of $S$ so that these normal vectors vary continuously over $S$. In other words, there is a continuous function $φ : S → \mathbb{R}3$ for which $||φ(u)|| = 1$ for all $u ∈ S$.

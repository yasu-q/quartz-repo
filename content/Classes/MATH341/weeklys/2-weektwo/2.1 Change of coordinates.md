## 2.1 Coordinate vectors
Let $B = ({\bf b}_1, {\bf b}_2, . . . , {\bf b}_n)$ be an ordered basis for a vector space $V$. The coordinate vector of a vector ${\bf v} \in V$ relative to $B$ is a column vector $(c_1. c_2 \dots, c_n)^T$ for which ${\bf v} = \sum_{j = 1}^{b}c_j{\bf b}_j$ . This vector is denoted by $[{\bf v}]_B$. The scalars $c_1, \dots , c_n$ are called the coordinates of ${\bf v}$ in basis $B$.

Like, under the standard basis for $\mathbb{R}^2$ which is $({\bf e}_1, {\bf e}_2)$ the coordinate vector for $(a, b)$ is just itself $(a, b)$ because $a{\bf e}_1 + b{\bf e}_2 = (a, b)$
## Theorem 2.1 
Let $B = ({\bf b}_1, \dots , {\bf b}_n)$ be an ordered basis for the vector space $V$ over ${\bf F}$. Then, the function $T : V → \mathbb{F}^n$ defined by $T ({\bf v}) = [{\bf v}]_B$ is an isomorphism
## Theorem 2.2
Let $V$ and $W$ be two vector spaces with ordered bases $A = ({\bf a}_1, {\bf a}_2, \dots , {\bf a}_n)$ and $B = ({\bf b}_1, {\bf b}_2, \dots , {\bf b}_m)$, respectively, and let $T : V → W$ be a linear transformation. Then, there is a unique $m \times n$ matrix $A$ for which 
$$
[T (v)]_B = A[{\bf v}]_A
$$
Furthermore, this matrix A can be obtained by the following formula
$$
A = ([T({\bf a}_1)]_B, \dots, [T({\bf a}_n)]_B)
$$
## Theorem 2.3
Suppose $U, V, W$ are three vector spaces with ordered bases $A, B$, and $C$, respectively. Let $S : U → V$ , and $T : V → W$ be linear transformations. Then, 
$$
[T \circ S]_{CA} = [T]_{CB}[S]_{BA}
$$
Now, consider the identity transformation $I : V → V$ defined by $I({\bf v}) = {\bf v}$, from a vector space $V$ to itself, and let $A, B$ be two ordered bases for $V$. We have 
$$
[{\bf v}]_B = [I]_{BA}[{\bf v}]_A
$$
This matrix $[I]_{BA}$ is called the matrix of change of coordinates from $A$ to $B$, because it allows us to change the coordinates of a vector from a basis $A$ to a basis $B$.

## Theorem 2.4
$$
[I]_{BA} = [I]_{AB}^{-1}
$$
The above theorem is especially useful when one of the bases is a standard basis. If $S$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb{F}^n$, then 
$$
[I]_{SA} = ({\bf a}_1 · · · {\bf a}_n)
$$
and thus
$$
[I]_{AS} = ({\bf a}_1 · · · {\bf a}_n)^{-1}
$$
